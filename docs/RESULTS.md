## 1. Autonomous trench-to-grade on the captured terrain

Method: `npm run bench -- <soil> <slope>` steps the app's own earthwork sim, dig-cycle controller and lidar model headlessly on the real terrain extracted from the capture (dt = 50 ms, both lidars at 10 Hz). Design: 3.6 m trench along the boom heading, 4.2 to 7.8 m from the swing centre, 0.9 m bottom width, 1.0 m deep. "Truth" = simulator state; "lidar" = the controller's own survey from noisy, occluded returns accumulated over a 1.6 s dwell.

| sandy-gravel | 1:1 | 2 | 32 | 19.07 s | 11% | 1.118 m³ | 34.5% | 5.34 cm | 0.331 m³ | 0.077 m³ | 2.062 m³ | 68% | 32% | -6.28 vs -6.57 cm | no |

Reading it: with a slope the soil can hold (wet clay at 45°, critical angle 62° in this model) the machine reaches grade in one pass and its lidar agrees with truth to well under a centimetre. With slopes steeper than the critical angle the walls slough (sandy gravel: 2 m³ of collapse), over-dig appears, and the controller's acceptance test (≥ 85% of measured cells within 1.5× tolerance, or no further improvement) refuses to sign off. The lidar estimate tracks truth within about half a centimetre in every run, including the failures.

Caveats: the soil model is a heightfield with repose/critical-angle rules, not continuum mechanics; soil parameters are textbook values, not site-measured; bucket-soil forces and hydraulics are not modelled.

## 2. Perception: camera vs lidar, by distance and condition

Method: in-app sweep (Scenario → Run perception sweep). Twin mode, so the machine is a mesh and occlusion by boom/arm/bucket is ray-tested exactly. A worker (mesh, hi-vis PPE) is placed at 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 15, 18, 22 m and bearings -28, -14, 0, 14° from the roof camera, in four conditions. Camera: YOLOX-Nano (COCO, 416, ONNX, WASM), person class, score ≥ 0.35, IoU ≥ 0.3 against the projected ground-truth box; frames where the worker is < 30% visible or < 8 px tall are excluded from camera recall. Lidar: ≥ 5 person-labelled returns across both units (a visibility measure, not a trained detector). Mean inference 68 ms/frame (WASM, single thread). Created 2026-11-07T07:15:14.577Z.

Reading it: the camera fails close in (people within ~5 m are seen nearly top-down from the 5 m mast, a viewpoint COCO-trained detectors rarely see) and far out (under ~24 px), and heavy dust removes it entirely. The two lidars together see the worker at every tested distance; the level roof unit alone has a near-field blind zone (0 returns at 4 to 6 m) that the tilted front unit covers. Separately, the blind-spot test places a worker 3.4 m from the swing centre beside the cab, where neither unit gets a single return.

Caveats: the worker is a mesh in a splat world, so this measures detection against neural-rendered backgrounds, not neural-rendered people; the dust and rain visuals are approximations (particles + colour grade), so the camera's weather sensitivity is indicative only; YOLOX-Nano is the smallest model in its family; sample sizes are small (3 to 4 frames per cell), so treat individual cells as indicative and the trends as the finding.

An earlier version of this sweep with the photographed (splat) machine in view produced a consistent false positive: COCO's person class fired on the splat-rendered boom (score ~0.5). It disappeared with the mesh twin, which is itself a small sim-to-real observation about splat artefacts.

## 3. Data export

A 4.2 s session recorded in the browser and checked with `npm run verify-mcap` (official `@mcap/core` indexed reader): 7 channels with Foxglove schemas (foxglove.PointCloud ×2, CompressedImage, FrameTransforms, Grid) plus joint and earthwork streams; roof scans ~16.8k points, front scans ~11.8k points, JPEG frames, 240×240 elevation grid.

## 4. Own reconstruction from the raw footage

Source: the original 4K drone video (Pexels 29568320), 200 frames at 1600 px. Camera poses with pycolmap: **200 / 200 frames registered, 82,411 points, 0.46 px mean reprojection error** (features 16 s, matching 13 s, mapping 208 s, CPU).

Training: Brush v0.3.0, 7000 steps on 175 frames (every 8th frame held out), 337 s on Apple Silicon. Held-out evaluation on 25 frames, recomputed independently from Brush's saved renders vs the source frames (1600×900): **PSNR 23.53 dB, SSIM 0.723, LPIPS-Alex 0.330** (per-frame PSNR 20.9 to 24.6 dB). Side-by-sides: `pipeline/train/heldout/`.

This is a short training run (the original 3DGS paper reports 30k iterations), so it is a baseline rather than a tuned number. One visible failure: the articulated dump truck driving through the footage renders as a smeared ghost, because 3DGS assumes a static scene. That is the same assumption LiveDirt's diggable terrain is built to break.
